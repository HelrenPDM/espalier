---
title: Checking and sharing generated text
position: 3
---

:::text{provenance=invented}
Before you use a generated text, you decide which of its statements need a check, whether the task was yours to hand over, and how your team learns that an assistant contributed.
:::

:::explanation{collapsed_on=short provenance=invented}
A generated statement can be fluent and still wrong. Such a [[term:hallucination]] looks like any other sentence. Names, numbers, dates and quotations therefore each need a [[term:source-check|source check]] before you use them, and asking the assistant again replaces no source.
:::

:::quote{provenance=sourced cite="office-study#Interviews"}
I now read every generated paragraph as if a new colleague had written it.
:::

:::callout{provenance=invented}
Some tasks stay with you. Assessments of people, decisions about applications and texts that contain [[term:personal-data|personal data]] need your own judgement, and the input rules of your organization decide what may go into a prompt.
:::

:::example{provenance=invented}
A team agrees to end every shared document that an assistant helped to draft with the line "Drafted with an AI assistant, checked by" and the initials of the person who checked it. Everyone who reads the document can then see where an assistant contributed and who answers for the content.
:::
